The fastest win

Emails, texts and follow-up writing

  • Post-showing thank-you. A check-in with a past client. A "still looking?" text.
  • The first draft takes seconds. You edit it, and you send it.
  • For most agents, this serves the people you already have. Follow-up is where that business comes from.
  • A follow-up assistant can suggest who to reach next and draft an opener. You make the call.
Today: copy the draft into the tool you already use. I will not guess at what Follow Up Boss's built-in AI can do.
Read the long stuff. Walk in ready.

Summaries, appointment prep and research (including buyers)

Summaries

Paste a long email, condo status certificate notes, or call notes. No private client identifiers. Ask for key points, risks, and questions. Then check the original.

Appointment prep

Ask for a one-page sheet for a first buyer consultation. Sample: a couple relocating to Midland.

Buyers

Neighbourhood questions, a showing-day checklist, the follow-up after, and a plain-language explainer. Example: what a home inspection condition means. Verify every fact.

Search stays in MLS

AI helps you prepare questions and summarize. It does not replace your search.

Sample prompt 
I'm a REALTOR® in Midland, Ontario. Here are my messy notes from a showing today. SAMPLE DATA ONLY.

Buyers: Alex and Jordan Sample. Saw 123 Maple Sample St. Loved the kitchen and the walk to the waterfront trail. Worried the yard is small for their dog. Asked about school bus routes and what similar homes sold for. They're pre-approved, want to decide in 2 weeks.

Please give me:
1. A short, friendly follow-up email I can send tonight (under 120 words, no em dashes, no exclamation marks).
2. Three questions to ask them on our next call.
3. A to-do list for me before that call, marking anything I need to verify myself.
Do not invent sale prices or school facts. Say "verify" where I need to check.

1. Email
Hi Alex and Jordan,

Thank you for walking through 123 Maple Sample St with me today. You loved the kitchen and the walk to the waterfront trail. You are also worried the yard is small for your dog.

You are pre-approved and want to decide in two weeks. I will verify school bus routes and what similar homes sold for before our next call. I will not guess at either.

Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®

2. Questions
- What would the yard need for your dog to be comfortable?
- Which school bus details should I verify before we talk?
- Once similar sales are verified, what else do you need in order to decide?

3. To-do before the call
- Verify school bus routes for this address. Do not guess.
- Verify what similar homes sold for. Do not invent prices.
- Confirm the yard size from the listing or a second look.
- Read the draft, fix one line if needed, and send it yourself.

Access, in plain language

Connecting your accounts: what it can do and what you're allowing

Week one: connect nothing

Copy the draft into Outlook, Follow Up Boss, or your texts. That covers most of the value.

If you connect later

A connector to email, calendar, or files may read, draft, or sometimes act. Read the permission screen before you allow it. Start with read-only or drafts only.

Never auto-send

You hit send. Keep MLS, TRREB, and client financial data out unless the brokerage approves.

Ask leadership first

Before any connection to Follow Up Boss, MLS, company email, or a transaction system. Add one connection at a time. A listing-paperwork assistant is a later role.

Which tool, and do I pay?

The one you will open every day. Free is enough to start. One plan later, if you are hitting limits.

Mistakes, and sending

It can be wrong with confidence. You verify, you read, and you send. Never auto-send.

Client information

Keep it out. Use Sample. No ID, banking, or mortgage numbers. Ask leadership before any connection.
